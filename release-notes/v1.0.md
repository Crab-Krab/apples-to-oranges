First release of Apples to Oranges: compare an original video with an encoded copy, frame by frame, in your browser.

**Use it online:** https://crab-krab.github.io/apples-to-oranges/
**Or offline:** download `index.html` below and double-click it. It is one self-contained file with nothing to install.

Your videos never leave your computer. The page opens them straight from your disk, and a built-in security policy blocks it from sending or loading anything over the network.

## What it does

- **Three views:** side by side, a wipe slider, and a difference view that lights up changed pixels (heatmap, grayscale or per-channel, with adjustable boost).
- **Frame-accurate:** both videos stay on exactly the same frame when paused, stepping or scrubbing, and stay in sync during playback from 0.1x to 2x.
- **Zoom to the pixel:** true 1:1 view at any screen scaling, with zoom and pan shared by every view.
- **Knows your files:** shows resolution, frame rate (measured automatically, up to 240 fps), length, size and codec, including the H.264/HEVC profile and bit depth.
- **Warns you** when the two files differ in resolution, aspect ratio, frame rate or length, and when a video can't show the frame that was asked for (a damaged or cut-off file, for example).
- **Explains playback failures:** when a file won't play, it shows what your browser supports and why, for example that 10-bit H.264 often fails in Firefox.
- **Keyboard friendly,** with shortcuts for everything (press `?` in the tool), and no issues found by an automated accessibility check.

## Browser support

Built for current Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Opera (only Chromium-based browsers are tested automatically). Which codecs play depends on the browser, the operating system and the graphics hardware. See the **Codecs** button in the tool, or the README's codec table.

## Known limitations

- Automated tests run in Chromium only. Firefox and Safari have had little testing.
- HDR and 10-bit video are shown through the browser's standard 8-bit output.
- Variable-frame-rate videos (common from phones) have approximate frame numbers.
- Very rarely, on a heavily loaded computer, pausing at full speed has left the Encoded video one frame off (seen once in automated testing, cause not yet found). If the two pictures look one frame apart after pausing, step one frame forward and back.

## Testing

106 automated browser tests run on every change, covering frame accuracy, pausing, frame-rate detection, the three views, pixel-exact 1:1, codec detection, accessibility and the network block.
